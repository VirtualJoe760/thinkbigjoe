# Cold email — the personal-first doctrine

**The old way is retired: we do NOT cold-send a claim code anymore.** A stranger opening "here's
your claim code TBJ-XXXX" reads it as automated spam — because it is. The claim-code send still
exists, but as a **manual option on the leads dashboard** (Joe fires it when a conversation has
earned it), never as the default first touch.

This doctrine comes from two sources, both primary:
1. **The Kyle Harebottle email (2026-07-26)** — a cold email that got Joe, a realtor who ignores
   hundreds of pitches, to reply within a day. Dissected below.
2. **Alex Hormozi's cold-outreach + email teaching** ($100M Leads "core four", his email tactics
   videos — transcripts reviewed 2026-07-26).

## Why Kyle's email worked (the model to imitate)

> "Hi, my name is Kyle, and I'm a small business owner based in Palm Desert. I created a local
> postcard advertising collaboration to help businesses reach more homeowners throughout the
> Coachella Valley. Each mailing reaches 10,000 local homes and features only 13 businesses, with
> just one business represented per industry.
> I'm currently looking to feature a local realtor. If you'd like exclusive representation for your
> industry and the opportunity to reach 10,000 local residents directly, I'd be happy to share more
> details.
> If you're interested, please email me at … or call/text me at …"

What it does, line by line:
1. **A named human, local peer.** "My name is Kyle, small business owner based in Palm Desert."
   Owner-to-owner, not company-to-lead. Locality is instant trust.
2. **Plain text. Zero links, zero images, zero tracking** in touch 1. Nothing for spam filters or
   skepticism to catch. (Hormozi says the ideal email "looks like emails you exchange every day.")
3. **Concrete and specific.** 10,000 homes · 13 businesses · one per industry. Numbers make it
   real; vague "grow your online presence" language is a conversion killer.
4. **Scarcity + exclusivity that's TRUE.** One realtor spot. Not fake urgency — the actual shape
   of the offer.
5. **The recipient is specifically wanted.** "I'm currently looking to feature a local realtor" —
   you, not one of 10,000 BCCs.
6. **Soft, low-friction CTA.** "I'd be happy to share more details" — asks for interest, not money,
   not a meeting slot. Reply, call, or text: their choice of channel.
7. **Held the pitch back.** Price, product photos, mechanics came only in reply #2, AFTER Joe
   engaged. Sell the conversation first; the offer sells on the second touch.
8. **Short.** ~110 words, third-grade reading level.

## Hormozi's rules we adopt

- **"Personalize, then give big fast value."** Make cold feel warm. Our unfair advantage: **we
  already built them something** — the preview site IS the big fast value. But we LEAD with the
  personal observation and the gift framing, not the code mechanics.
- **Personalization = proof a human looked.** Their reviews ("saw Maria called you the only honest
  AC guy in Mesa"), their trade, their town, a competitor going digital. NEVER a naked
  `{{first_name}}` merge. "This is also just called being a human being" — 30 seconds of actually
  looking.
- **Write below 3rd-grade reading level.** Hormozi: rewriting templates simpler = **+50% response**.
  Short words, short sentences, one idea.
- **One CTA, low friction.** A question they can answer from their truck, not a calendar link.
- **Multi-channel, persistent follow-up.** Email + SMS + voicemail are one conversation (our SMS
  cadence already does 2×/week; email follow-ups interleave, new angle every touch). "Contact
  someone multiple times and you show you're serious."
- **On replies, ACA:** **A**cknowledge what they said → **C**ompliment tied to it → **A**sk a
  question that leads toward the call. Never jump from their reply to the pitch.
- **Deliverability is sacred** (compounding asset, one domain): plain text · no images · **max one
  link, and only when earned** · no money-language in subject/body ("free", "guarantee", "$$") ·
  easy opt-out honored instantly · replies are gold (they train inboxes to trust us — end with a
  question that invites one).
- **Subject lines:** lowercase, curiosity or specificity, zero salesiness. Good: `question about
  <business name>` · `your reviews in <city>` · `<city> homeowners`. Bad: "FREE website!",
  anything with their claim code in it.
- **Timing:** B2B opens peak **Wednesday**; windows **10–12** and **1–3** local time. Send inside
  the existing weekday working-hours window.
- **Track it:** sends → replies → calls booked → closes, per angle/template. Kill what doesn't
  reply, scale what does (we optimize on replies + booked calls, never opens).

## The offer (2026-07-26, Joe's call): voice-led, price named up front

**The AI receptionist is the product; the website is the free bonus.** This is the existing $299/mo
Website + Voice tier, reframed as a grand-slam offer (Hormozi: stack value, anchor the price,
make the bonus feel stupid to leave):

> **"We have multiple affordable plans starting at $99/mo plus a modest fee for the site. For a
> couple hundred more a month you never miss a phone call again — our AI receptionist answers your
> calls and books appointments for you. We also have AI agents that generate leads and make sales."**

(2026-07-26 revision, Joe's wording: soft-anchor at $99, "a couple hundred more" instead of naming
$299, capabilities-forward. CTA now proposes a concrete Zoom time + the calendar link
(/book-appointment) + the concierge line (480) 764-2121. **Sign as "Joe"** — the from-address is
joe@thinkbigjoe.com, so any alias would mismatch the mailbox and read fake. The "Mark" alias
experiment (2026-07-27) was reverted the same day for exactly that reason.)

Why price goes IN the first email now: it qualifies instantly (no wasted threads with people who'd
never spend), it reads confident instead of bait-y, and the anchor works for us — a site alone is
$99/mo, so "free with voice" is a real, checkable discount. Kyle named his number in message 2;
we're one step more direct because our number IS the hook ($3,000 of agency work for $299 —
the same $3,000→$400 contrast that made Kyle's postcard land).

## The TBJ first-touch shape (email)

Structure — 60–120 words, plain text:
1. **Human intro, local when true:** who's writing, from where, owner-energy.
2. **The personal observation** (the real one): their reviews / years in business / a specific
   thing from their listing.
3. **The gift + the offer:** we made a free PREVIEW; if they like it we build the full site and
   they customize everything. Pricing in Joe's approved frame (plans start $99/mo + modest site
   fee; a couple hundred more = the AI receptionist answering + booking; AI agents for leads +
   sales too). One business per trade per area.
4. **CTA:** propose a concrete Zoom time + the calendar link (/book-appointment) + "or call
   (480) 764-2121 and our concierge will book you in." Sign as **Joe** (matches the from-address).

Example (handyman, Chandler):

> Hi Gene — Joe with ThinkBigJoe, a Web & AI agency. I noticed your 4.9★ reviews, so we made
> Ready To Sell AZ a free website preview — it's below. If you like it, we build the full site
> and you customize everything. Plans start at $99/mo — and for a bit more, our AI receptionist
> answers every call and books your jobs.
>
> Worth a quick Zoom this week? Grab a time: thinkbigjoe.com/book-appointment — or call
> (480) 764-2121 and our concierge will book you in.

**~70 words. Short wins** (Joe, 2026-07-26): three beats — who/why · the offer · the ask — no filler.

Note what's absent: **no claim code, no "free website" as the headline** (free-with-voice is the
bonus framing, never "we do free websites"). The link goes out when they say "sure" — that reply
is engagement, trains deliverability, and makes touch 2 a conversation. The claim code appears only
when they've seen the site and want it — or when Joe manually fires the claim-code email from the
leads dashboard (the legacy sender, now an operator tool).

## Follow-up (email leg of the one conversation)

Same cadence philosophy as SMS (fresh angle every touch, stop on reply/claim/booking/opt-out):
- Touch 2: a specific thing on their site ("put your 'same-day' promise front and center — that's
  what your reviews rave about").
- Touch 3: a curiosity nudge ("the site's still reserved for you — one <trade> per area is the
  rule. still want a look?").
- Later touches rotate: a review compliment → gap · what a missed 7pm call costs · "did the link
  even reach you?" — never "just checking in."

## 2026-07-26 addendum: the outreach autopsy + the transparent SMS first touch

**The numbers to date (why the copy changed):** 2,624 leads · 183 voicemails dropped (only 68
confirmed delivered — 37%) · **210 SMS first-touched → 6 replies (2.9%) → 0 meetings, 0 sales** ·
24 emails → 0 replies · 2 claims, neither converted. The one hostile reply we got named the
problem exactly: *"You didn't make shit for me, scammer."* — **"I built you a website" from an
unknown number is the single most common local-biz scam opener.** The claim was also an overclaim
(it's a preview, not their finished site), and people feel that on click.

**The fix (Joe's call + Hormozi + the Payton/loom model):** radical transparency. It's a
**preview**; their OK triggers the full build; then they customize. This matches the loom-audit
outreach model (value-first artifact → honest framing → follow-up conversation) and Hormozi's
"offer what other people actually charge for" (a site design that agencies bill thousands for —
free to look at, named as such).

New SMS first-touch shape (all three variants in `src/lib/forge-outreach.ts`):
> Hi, this is Joe with ThinkBigJoe, a Web & AI agency. Saw <Business>'s great reviews but
> no website, so we made a free preview of what one could look like: <link> — if you like it, we
> build the full site and you can customize everything. Worth a look? (Not interested? Just reply
> 'No thanks' and I'll stop.)

What changed: named studio identity (not bare "Joe") · "preview of what one could look like"
(honest) · the pipeline stated (your OK → full build → you customize) · question CTA ("worth a
look?") · opt-out kept. The email composer, SMS agent, outreach cron, and agent personas all carry
the same framing — the word "preview" is now load-bearing everywhere.

**Hormozi extras adopted from the cold-DM chapter:** match the local area code on the sending
number where possible · sub-3rd-grade reading level on every script · "you'll learn more from your
first 100 sends than 100 books" — ship, measure reply-rate per variant, iterate. SMS-practitioner
norms confirmed: mobile-verified numbers only, watch the opt-out rate (>3% = carrier flags),
which our number-warming caps already respect.
