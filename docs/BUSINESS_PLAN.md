## The business in five lines

1. We sell an **AI operations system** to home service businesses.
2. The **voice agent** is what they buy first — it answers every call and books the job.
3. The **portal** is where all of it manifests, and it is what makes us hard to replace.
4. More **agents** turn on over time from a fixed menu. That's how revenue grows without new customers.
5. A **website** is included when theirs is bad. It costs us nothing and it closes deals.

**Brand note:** "ThinkBigJoe" addresses the customer — *think big, Joe.* It is not a person's name.
Do not build the brand around Joseph Sardella.

## Who we sell to

Owner-operated home service companies: **plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing, garage door,
restoration, towing, locksmith, pest control, auto repair.**

Qualifiers: the phone is the cash register · one job is worth $300+ · the owner decides alone ·
findable on Google Maps.

**These businesses are demand-rich.** They are not short of customers — they are short of capacity
and follow-through. **Never pitch lead generation.** Pitch stopping the leaks.

## What we sell

### The portal (always included, never negotiable)

Where the agents' work becomes visible: calls answered, jobs booked, revenue recovered, and a
monthly ROI number. Only 18% of businesses track AI ROI at all — handing them the number every
month is both the retention mechanism and the renewal conversation.

**This is the moat.** Prompt-level agents retain 80–85% because anyone can swap them out. Systems
that own the interface, the data and the workflow retain 93–97%. A phone bot is portable. A portal
holding a year of their call history and customer list is not.

### The agent menu

| Tier | Agents | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Answer** | Voice: answers, qualifies, books | **$497/mo** |
| **2 — Respond** | + text/SMS handling, unsold estimate follow-up | **$797/mo** |
| **3 — Recover** | + seasonal reminders, reactivation, review requests | **$1,197/mo** |

$250 setup. Month-to-month. **Land at tier 1, expand on the portal** — they can see the other
agents switched off.

**Roadmap agents** (not sold yet): membership enrollment/renewal, collections, technician recruiting.
Recruiting is the sleeper — if demand is strong, labor is the binding constraint, and nobody is
selling that to them.

### Why the agents are what they are

Demand is not their problem, so every agent points at money already in the building:

- **Unsold estimate follow-up** — the biggest leak in home services. A quoted $8,000 replacement
  nobody called back about. The only agent that starts with a known dollar figure attached.
- **Seasonal reminders** — spring AC, fall furnace. Their existing list, twice a year, zero CAC.
- **Reactivation** — thousands of past customers sitting dead in their CRM.
- **Reviews** — drives local rank, so more demand arrives as a byproduct.

### The hard rule on scope

**The agent menu is fixed. It is not "we'll build what you need."**

The moment it becomes custom work, this is a consultancy with one employee, and hours-per-account
climbs past the point where it is a product. Off-menu requests go on a roadmap and ship to
everyone or not at all. **New agents are built once, for all accounts, on our schedule.**

## How we sell it

The demo is a phone number. No deck, no screen share, nothing to schedule. That is the entire
reason this can close in days instead of quarters.

**The loop:**

1. **Scrape** 50 businesses/day from Maps.
2. **Call test** each one after 6pm or Sunday morning. Log: answered / voicemail / rang out.
   **Only the failures become prospects.**
3. **Voicemail drop + follow-up text** to every failure, opening with what we measured:
   > *"I called your line at 7:40 Tuesday night and it rang out. I run a service that answers
   > those calls and books the job. Want to hear it? Call [number] and talk to it yourself."*
4. **They call the demo line.** That's the demo.
5. **Close on the callback.** Month-to-month, no contract.
6. **Live in 48 hours** — they forward after-hours calls; we configure from scraped data.

**Channel rules:**

| Channel | Use |
|---|---|
| Human-dialed phone | Primary — the qualifier and the closer |
| Ringless voicemail + text | First touch, after the call test |
| Email | Secondary, once domains are warm (3 weeks) |
| SMS | **Post-engagement only** — never cold. Compliance and carrier filtering. |
| Voice agent | **Inbound demo only** — never cold outbound dialing |

**Cold outbound domains** (protect the root, warm 3 weeks): `thinkbigjoe.co` · `pipelinedept.com` ·
`meetingsdept.com`.

## The math

50 calls/day → ~20 fail to answer → voicemail + text those 20 → 2–3 callbacks/day →
**close roughly 1 every 2–3 days.**

At $497 + $250 setup: **10–15 customers in month one ≈ $5–7.5K MRR** plus setup fees.

Then expansion does the rest — moving a tier-1 account to tier 3 is +$700/mo with no new sale.

## Risks

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| **Commodity pressure** — Rosie $49, self-serve tools | Win on done-for-you setup + the portal, never on price. If losing on price, go multi-location, don't discount. |
| **Scope creep into custom work** | Fixed menu. Track hours/account weekly. |
| **Churn on the portable layer** | Every account must have real history in the portal within 60 days. That's the switching cost. |
| **Selling agents that don't exist yet** | Sell tier 1 only until tiers 2–3 are actually built. Never promise a date. |
| **SMS/voice compliance** | No cold SMS. No cold AI dialing. Human dials business lines only. |

## Where this goes

Single-location voice is the on-ramp, not the destination. Every customer becomes a case study with
a number: *"answered 47 after-hours calls, booked 12 jobs, month one."*

That proof is what makes a 6-location group worth $2,400/month a winnable conversation — which it
is not today, because we have zero references. **Land single-location now, earn multi-location next.**
